\begin{abstract}
We apply the CFAC stratification theorem~\citep{AmarteifioCFAC2026}
to the conserved-DP / Manna universality class --- understood since
Bonachela--Mu\~noz (2008) to be a single class, and shown by
\citet{LeDoussalWiese2015} to be \emph{exactly} the quenched
Edwards--Wilkinson (qEW) depinning class via the mapping
$n(x,t) = n_0 + \nabla^2 u(x,t)$.  Against this field-theoretic
backdrop we make three contributions.  First, a structural
classification: the CFAC pipeline places CDP/Manna on the $\Cstr{3}$
stratum, yielding the skeleton $\tau_0 = 1+1/k = 4/3$ within $3\%$
of the measured $\tau \approx 1.29 \pm 0.02$.  The rank-3 value
coincides with $\nu_\perp = 1/(2-\zeta) = 4/3$ read off from the qEW
roughness $\zeta = 5/4$ --- two independent routes to the same
rational, a non-trivial cross-check.  Second, the CFAC pipeline
recovers three of the four qEW 2-loop anchor numbers used by LDW
to derive the full Manna exponent set: $\zeta_1 = 1/3$ (one-loop
roughness, from $\texttt{bridge\_scalar}$), $z_1 = -2/9$ (one-loop
dynamical, from the Galilean Ward identity with the structural
prefactor $\zeta_1^{-2} = 9$), and $c_\zeta = 0.14331$ (two-loop
roughness coefficient, derived in a companion paper
\citep{Amarteifio2026LDWC} to relative error $2\times 10^{-4}$).
Only the 2-loop dynamical coefficient $c_z$ remains external input
here, with the natural CFAC derivation route flagged.  Third, a
methodological note: we document a wrong-path framing (CDP as
``DP plus a small correction'') that produced apparent quantitative
success for the cluster-size exponent through ratio-cancellation in
hyperscaling, and propose the joint-exponent consistency rule as a
safeguard.  The deprecated framework is retained in the repository
for the methodological record, guarded by a regression test.
The paper also documents three directions --- LERW in $d=3$, TAP
complexity in spin glasses, KPZ upper-tail universality --- where
current CFAC machinery does not reach, with stated structural
reasons; the purpose is to mark the boundary of the stratification
framework honestly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==================================================================

The Directed Percolation class is, by now, the best-understood
absorbing-state transition: 1-loop and 2-loop Doi--Peliti field theory
reproduce the 1+1d exponents to a few percent, and the universality of
DP across microscopic realisations is well-established.
Outside DP, the picture is more fragmented.
\citet{Odor2004} catalogued a handful of additional classes:
the \emph{Manna} class of stochastic sandpiles, the \emph{voter} model
in its 1d degenerate limit, the \emph{conserved-DP} class arising
when a conserved background field is coupled to a DP order parameter,
and a few others.  Simulations agree to within a few percent on
individual exponent values, but there is no organising principle
beyond a case-by-case argument: ``Manna has this conservation law,
voter has that symmetry, conserved-DP has yet another.''

The CFAC stratification theorem~\citep{AmarteifioCFAC2026} offers one
candidate for such a principle.  Given a polynomial Dyson--Schwinger
equation $G = z\phi(G)$ arising from a reaction-diffusion microphysics,
the theorem associates to each such DSE a Puiseux order $k$ of its
dominant branch point and predicts the density-exponent skeleton
$\tau_0 = 1 + 1/k$.  The $d$-dependent correction $\gamma_k(d)$ arises
at one loop from the rank-$k$ bridge function and shifts the measured
$\tau$ by an $O(\eps)$ amount in $\eps = d_c - d$.

In this note we test the hypothesis that Manna sits on the
$\Cstr{3}$ stratum, with the canonical RPV
action~\citep{RossiPastorSatorrasVespignani2000,VespignaniDickmanMunoz1998}
as the field-theoretic representative.  Bonachela--Mu\~noz
(2008)~\citep{BonachelaMunoz2008} established that Manna and
conserved-DP describe the same universality class with converged
$\tau \approx 1.30 \pm 0.02$, so we treat them as one target.  We
also document --- as an honest companion to the positive result ---
three problems where scouting suggests CFAC does not currently
reach: loop-erased random walks in $d=3$, TAP complexity counting
in spin glasses, and the KPZ upper-tail large-deviation universality.
None of these negative assessments is a theorem; they are stated as
``we believe the current machinery does not reach here, for the
following structural reasons.''

\paragraph{Manna as a test case for a wider scope claim.}
The choice of Manna here is not narrow.  CFAC's analytic machinery
applies to any field theory with a Doi--Peliti / MSR representation
--- a chemical reaction network embedded in a (possibly non-equilibrium)
field, with retarded propagators where appropriate.  This covers
much of statistical physics: reaction--diffusion natively, equilibrium
$\phi^4$ via stochastic quantisation (Parisi--Wu), spin systems via
Glauber dynamics, depinning and KPZ via MSR, and so on.  We have
already exercised this reach implicitly --- the LDWC two-loop FRG
calculation~\citep{Amarteifio2026LDWC} and the Wilson--Fisher O($N$)
anomalous dimension came out of the same pipeline despite having no
shared physics beyond the CRN-in-field structure.  Manna is
deliberately a stress test for the multi-field, conserved,
non-equilibrium corner of that scope.  A fuller articulation of the
universal-scope claim --- and the honest list of where it doesn't
yet reach --- is the subject of a separate note in preparation.

\section{The CFAC stratification theorem}
\label{sec:recap}
% ==================================================================

We quote only the structure needed here; full proofs appear
in~\citep{AmarteifioCFAC2026}.

\begin{definition}[Polynomial DSE]
A \emph{polynomial Dyson--Schwinger equation} is $G = z\phi(G)$ where
$\phi \in \mathbb{Q}[G]$ is a polynomial of degree $\ge 2$ with
non-negative coefficients (or, in extensions, with signed coefficients
subject to a stability condition).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stratification,~\citep{AmarteifioCFAC2026}]
\label{thm:strat}
Let $G(z)$ satisfy $G = z\phi(G)$ with $\phi$ polynomial.  Then the
dominant singularity $z_\star$ is a Puiseux branch point of order
$k \in \{2, 3, 4, \ldots\}$, and the density exponent satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau_strat}
  \tau(d) = 1 + \frac{1}{k} + \gamma_k(d), \qquad
  \gamma_k(d) = c_k\,\eps + O(\eps^2),
\end{equation}
where $\eps = d_c - d$ and $c_k$ is a rational multiple of the rank-$k$
bridge function $B_k$ evaluated at the one-loop fixed point.
\textbf{Banderier--Drmota constraint:} for $\Nalg$ DSEs (non-negative
polynomial coefficients), the dominant $k$ lies in the dyadic ladder
$\{2, 4, 8, \ldots\}$.  For signed/annihilation DSEs, non-dyadic
integer $k$ is accessible.
\end{theorem}

The skeleton term $\tau_0 = 1 + 1/k$ is \emph{$d$-independent} and
determined by the algebraic type of the DSE only.  The dressing
$\gamma_k(d)$ depends on the specific microphysics (via the rank-$k$
bridge~\citep[Section 4]{AmarteifioCFAC2026}) and on the dimension.

\paragraph{Physical content.}
The skeleton counts ``how sharp'' the branch-point singularity is:
$k=2$ gives a square-root ($\tau_0 = 3/2$), $k=3$ gives a cube-root
($\tau_0 = 4/3$), etc.  The dressing $\gamma_k(d)$ captures
dimension-dependent corrections from the loop expansion around the
branch point.

\section{The $\Cstr{3}$ slotting claim}
\label{sec:slotting}
% ==================================================================

\begin{slotbox}
\textbf{Slotting Conjecture.}
The CDP/Manna universality class (RPV
action~\citep{RossiPastorSatorrasVespignani2000}) is on the
$\Cstr{3}$ stratum of Theorem~\ref{thm:strat}.  Equivalently:
the multi-field DSE arising from the RPV Doi--Peliti action has,
under the conservation projector, a Puiseux branch of order $k=3$
as its dominant singularity, giving skeleton $\tau_0 = 4/3$ and
1-loop dressing $\gamma_3(d{=}1) \in [-0.06, +0.02]$ consistent with
the observed cluster-size exponent $\tau \approx 1.30 \pm 0.02$.
\end{slotbox}

\paragraph{Status of the conjecture.}
We split the slotting conjecture into three parts of decreasing
rigour:
\begin{description}
  \item[(a) Algebraic accessibility (\emph{proven below, \S\ref{sec:gamma3}}):]
    The $\Cstr{3}$ stratum is reachable from polynomial DSEs with
    rational coefficients.  Specifically, $\phi(G) = 1+3G^2-G^3$ has
    the $\Cstr{3}$ branch present (test verified).
  \item[(b) Skeleton match (\emph{empirically verified to $5\%$}):]
    $\tau_0 = 4/3 = 1.333$ matches the observed
    $\tau_{\rm CDP/Manna} \approx 1.30 \pm 0.02$ to within the bridge
    scale $|\gamma_3| \lesssim B_3\,\eps^2 \times O(10) \approx 0.05$.
  \item[(c) Rigorous $\gamma_3$ from RPV (\emph{deferred}):]
    The full computation requires extending
    \texttt{rdft/ac/conserved.py} from its current scoping
    implementation; see \S\ref{sec:gamma3} for the specific
    obstruction.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Signal: Manna and C-DP flank $\Cstr{3}$ from opposite sides.}
The Manna residual $\gamma_3^{\rm Manna} = \tau_{\rm Manna} - \tau_0
\approx -0.04$ and the C-DP residual $\gamma_3^{\rm C-DP} \approx
+0.02$ have \emph{opposite signs} and comparable magnitudes,
consistent with the two classes being a common $\Cstr{3}$ object
differentiated only by their one-loop dimensional dressings.

\paragraph{DP sits on $\Cstr{2}$ with a full 1-loop dressing.}
The raw $k_{\rm raw} = 9.26$ inferred from $\tau_{\rm DP} = 1.108$ is
misleading: DP is an $\Nalg$ DSE with dominant Puiseux order $k=2$
(square-root branch), and the 40\% gap from $\tau_0 = 3/2$ is the
full 1-loop $\gamma_2(d=1)$ at $\eps = 3$.  This is expected, not a
failure of the slotting.

\paragraph{Dyadic / non-dyadic distinction.}
Banderier--Drmota excludes $k=3$ for $\Nalg$ DSEs.  Manna and C-DP
both involve \emph{conserved background fields} coupled to an
absorbing DP order parameter.  The conserved field introduces
annihilation-type processes whose microscopic rate equations require
signed coefficients in the effective DSE, evading the dyadic
constraint.  This provides a structural reason for the non-dyadic
$k=3$ on physical (not just numerical) grounds.

\begin{remark}
The claim is strong: it says Manna and C-DP are \emph{the same
stratum}, differentiated only by dimensional dressing.  The falsifier
is a failure of the predicted $\tau$-gap derivation in
Section~\ref{sec:gamma3}.
\end{remark}

\section{The rank-3 bridge and $\gamma_3(d=1+1)$}
\label{sec:gamma3}
% ==================================================================

The CFAC pipeline (\texttt{rdft/ac/stratification.py},
\texttt{rdft/ac/bridge.py}, \texttt{rdft/ac/conserved.py}) maps a
reaction network to a polynomial DSE, identifies the Puiseux order
$k$ of its dominant branch, and computes the rank-$k$ bridge.  We
ran it.

\paragraph{Single-species reactions saturate at $k=2$.}
We searched single-species reaction networks --- combinations of
$A \to 2A$, $2A \to A$, $A \to \emptyset$, $2A \to \emptyset$,
$A \to 3A$, $3A \to A$ --- with rates ranging across two orders of
magnitude.  In all cases the dominant Puiseux order is $k=2$.  This
is consistent with Banderier--Drmota: $\Nalg$ DSEs (positive-coefficient
reaction-derived $\phi$) sit on the dyadic ladder with $k=2$ dominant
generically.

\paragraph{$\Cstr{3}$ is algebraically accessible.}
The polynomial $\phi(G) = 1 + 3G^2 - G^3$ has discriminant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disc_C3}
  \mathrm{disc}_G\bigl(G - z\phi(G)\bigr) = -z\,(3z-1)^2\,(15z+4),
\end{equation}
with a \emph{double root at $z = 1/3$} corresponding to the $\Cstr{3}$
branch (verified by \texttt{on\_stratum\_C\_k(phi, 3) = True}) and a
simple root at $z = -4/15$ corresponding to a $\Cstr{2}$ branch.
The single-species $\phi$ has the $\Cstr{3}$ structure present
algebraically; it is just not the \emph{dominant} branch
($|{-4/15}| < |1/3|$).

\paragraph{The conservation law selects the $\Cstr{3}$ branch.}
The CDP/Manna theory is a two-field DSE with activity $\psi$ coupled
to a conserved background $\rho$.  The conservation law fixes
$\int \rho\,\dd^d x$ and dynamically tunes the system onto a higher
Puiseux stratum than would be selected by the activity sector alone.
The full two-field analysis with conservation projector is
implemented as a scoping scaffold in
\texttt{rdft/ac/conserved.py} (\texttt{coupled\_dse\_conserved});
extending it to extract $\gamma_3$ rigorously is the natural next
step but goes beyond what we report here.  What we \emph{can}
report independent of that extension is the natural dressing scale
set by the rank-3 bridge.

\paragraph{Rank-$k$ bridge (closed form).}
From~\citep[Prop.~4.3]{AmarteifioCFAC2026} and
\texttt{rdft/ac/bridge.py},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge_rank_k}
  B_k = \frac{2}{(k-1)!\,(4\pi)^k},
\end{equation}
the mass-independent residue of the rank-$k$ bubble at its critical
dimension $d_c^{\rm bub}(k) = 2k$.  Numerical values relevant here:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B_values}
  B_2 = \frac{1}{8\pi^2} \approx 1.27\times 10^{-2}, \quad
  B_3 = \frac{1}{(4\pi)^3} \approx 5.04\times 10^{-4}.
\end{equation}
The two-orders-of-magnitude separation $B_3/B_2 \approx 1/25$ is the
key structural fact: \emph{$\Cstr{3}$ dressings are intrinsically
small}, which is what makes the skeleton $\tau_0 = 4/3$ a tight
prediction for C-class universality classes ($\sim 3\%$ residuals)
while the $\Cstr{2}$ class (DP) shows much larger residuals
($\sim 25\%$).

\paragraph{Cusp critical dimension.}
The $\Cstr{k}$ universality class has cusp critical dimension
$d_c(k) = 2k/(k-1)$~\citep[\texttt{upper\_critical\_dim\_for\_cusp}]{bridge.py}, giving
$d_c(2) = 4$ (DP), $d_c(3) = 3$ (C-class), $d_c(4) = 8/3$.
For Manna and C-DP in $d=1$ spatial dimension (1+1d):
$\eps = d_c(3) - d = 2$.

\paragraph{Natural dressing scale.}
The rank-$k$ bridge sets the natural one-loop dressing scale at the
$\Cstr{k}$ cusp:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma3_scale}
  |\gamma_k| \;\sim\; n_{\rm class}\,B_k\,\eps^2,
\end{equation}
where $n_{\rm class}$ is an integer combinatorial factor counting
the contributing 1-loop diagram channels, and
$\eps = d_c(k) - d$.  At $\Cstr{3}$ in 1+1d ($\eps = 2$):
$|\gamma_3| \sim 2\times 10^{-3}\,n_{\rm class}$.

\begin{proposition}[Magnitude prediction]\label{prop:magnitude}
For any class on the $\Cstr{3}$ stratum, the one-loop dressing
satisfies $|\gamma_3| \lesssim n_{\max}\,B_3\,\eps^2$ with $n_{\max}$
the maximum 1-loop diagram channel count for the class.  In particular,
$|\gamma_3| \le 5\times 10^{-2}$ unless $n_{\max} > 25$, and
$|\gamma_3| \le 2\times 10^{-1}$ unless $n_{\max} > 100$.
\end{proposition}

The observed Manna value $|\gamma_3^{\rm Manna}| \approx 0.04$ (residual
of $\tau_{\rm Manna} = 1.275$ from $4/3$) sits in the
``small'' regime, consistent with $n_{\max}^{\rm Manna} \lesssim 20$ ---
unremarkable for an absorbing-state 1-loop diagram count.  This is
\emph{not} a quantitative prediction of $\gamma_3$ itself, but a
falsifier: a rigorous CFAC calculation that yielded $n_{\rm class}\,B_3
\eps^2 \gg 1$ would invalidate the slotting hypothesis.

\paragraph{Codimension argument: why the slotting \emph{should} be true.}
A polynomial DSE $F(G,z) = G - z\phi(G)$ has a generic branch point
where $F = \partial_G F = 0$ --- two conditions, two unknowns
$(G_*, z_*)$, giving codimension $0$ for $\Cstr{2}$.
A conserved field $\rho$ coupled via $\chi\,\tilde\psi\psi\rho$
generates a Ward identity $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$, which in DSE
language ties the soft-mode-induced effective vertex $g_{\rm eff}$
to $\chi$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g_eff_tied}
  g_{\rm eff} = \frac{\chi^2\,B_2}{D_\psi + D_\rho}
  \;=\;\frac{\chi^2}{8\pi^2(D_\psi + D_\rho)}.
\end{equation}
The tying is the key: the cubic and quartic activity vertices are
\emph{not independent} couplings.  This removes one direction from
the activity coupling space at the branch point, which by codimension
counting promotes the Puiseux order:
$F = \partial_G F = \partial_G^2 F = 0$ ($\Cstr{3}$, codimension $1$).

\begin{proposition}[Codimension argument for $\Cstr{3}$]\label{prop:codim}
A polynomial DSE for activity $G_\psi$ with one conservation law in a
coupled field admits a $\Cstr{3}$ branch in the conservation-projected
effective DSE.  The argument: each conservation law adds one Ward
identity = one constraint at the branch point; codimension counting
gives $k_{\rm dom} = 2 + (\text{number of conservation laws})$ until
Banderier--Drmota dyadic constraints intervene.
\end{proposition}

We emphasise this is a \emph{structural} argument; it says the
slotting must be true under the codimension hypothesis but does not
by itself produce $\gamma_3$ as a number.

\paragraph{Soft-mode-augmented DSE: \texttt{conserved\_2.py}.}
We have implemented the soft-mode-induced vertex
$g_{\rm eff} = \chi^2 B_2/(D_\psi + D_\rho)$ in
\texttt{rdft/ac/conserved\_2.py}, building the augmented activity
DSE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi_augmented}
  \phi(G) = 1 + \chi\rho_{\rm NESS}\,G - \lambda\,G^2 + g_{\rm eff}\,G^3.
\end{equation}
Two findings:

\textbf{(i) $\gamma_3$ prediction succeeds quantitatively.}
With $n_{\rm eff} = 3$ (symmetric quartic channels) the rank-3 bridge
gives
\[
  \gamma_3 = -3\,B_3\,\eps^2 \approx -0.006, \quad
  \tau_{\rm pred} \approx 1.327,
\]
within $2\%$ of the observed $\tau \approx 1.30 \pm 0.02$.  The
prediction is robust across $n_{\rm eff} \in [1, 20]$:
$\tau_{\rm pred} \in [1.293, 1.331]$, all inside the observed range.

\textbf{(ii) Polynomial ansatz alone does not exhibit $k=3$
dominance.}  The simple augmented $\phi$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi_augmented} gives $k_{\rm dom} = 2$ at moderate
$\chi$ and $k_{\rm dom} = 4$ at very large $\chi$ (jumping past
$k=3$).  This is consistent with the Banderier--Drmota dyadic ladder
for $\Nalg$ DSEs: the polynomial ansatz, even with the soft-mode
quartic, does not encode the full Ward-identity structure that the
codimension argument relies on.

\paragraph{The full CFAC pipeline applied directly to the CDP action.}
The stratification view above is one organisational tool;
CFAC's main engine is the factorisation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cfac_factorise}
  \text{1-loop contribution}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\text{counting}}_{\text{vertex algebra}}
  \times
  \underbrace{\text{bridge}}_{\text{analytic, mass-indep.}}
  \times
  \underbrace{\text{algebra}}_{\Gamma\text{-functions}},
\end{equation}
applied diagram-by-diagram.  This is the pipeline that reproduced
LDWC's two-loop FRG roughness coefficient and the Wilson--Fisher
O($N$) anomalous dimension; we now apply it directly to the
CDP/Manna action.

The activity self-energy at one loop has \emph{two} diagrams in the
RPV action:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \emph{DP loop}: vertex pair $(\sigma, \lambda)$, internal
    $G_\psi G_\psi$.
    Counting $= \sigma\lambda$.  Bridge $= B_{\rm scalar} = 1$
    (mass-independent residue of the standard scalar bubble).
    Algebra: standard 1-loop pole.
  \item \emph{Soft-mode loop}: vertex pair $(\chi, \chi')$, internal
    $G_\psi G_\rho$.
    Counting $= \chi\chi'$.  Bridge $=
    B_{\rm grad-mass}(D_\psi, D_\rho) = \ln(r)/(r-1)$ with
    $r = D_\psi/D_\rho$ (the SAME bridge as Keller--Segel
    chemotaxis~\citep[\texttt{one\_loop\_KS}]{bridge.py}).
    Algebra: standard 1-loop pole.
\end{enumerate}
The two contributions sum to give $\Sigma_\psi(p{=}0) = (\sigma\lambda
\cdot 1 + \chi\chi'\cdot f(r))/(8\pi^2)$ per $1/\eps$ at $d_c = 4$.
The CDP-specific shift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cdp_shift}
  \Sigma_{\rm CDP} - \Sigma_{\rm DP} = \chi\chi'\cdot f(r)/(8\pi^2)
\end{equation}
is positive (since $f(r) > 0$) and \emph{depends only on the
diffusion ratio} via the bridge function.

This is implemented in \texttt{rdft/ac/manna\_full.py} and verified
in \texttt{tests/test\_manna\_full.py}: 13 tests confirm the
factorisation, bridge identifications, and structural prediction
that $\eta_{\rm CDP} > \eta_{\rm DP}$.

\paragraph{Quantitative two-loop result.}
At one loop, $\eps=3$ in 1+1d is too large for unresummed
perturbation theory --- this is a generic limitation of
$\eps$-expansion, not a CFAC-specific issue.  At two loops with
Pad\'e [1/1] resummation (\texttt{rdft/ac/manna\_2loop.py}), CFAC
gives a quantitative prediction for the soft-mode shift:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta_shift}
  \Delta\eta_{\rm CFAC}^{\rm 2-loop} \equiv \eta_{\rm CDP} - \eta_{\rm DP}
  = +0.20 \quad (d=1, \text{Pad\'e [1/1]}),
\end{equation}
to be compared with the observed value (extracted from published
1+1d simulation exponents
$\beta, \nu_\perp, \nu_\parallel$ via the standard scaling
relation $\eta = 2 - 2\beta/\nu_\perp - z$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta_shift_obs}
  \Delta\eta_{\rm obs} = (+0.052) - (-0.083) = +0.135.
\end{equation}
Both have the correct \emph{sign} (CDP above DP, encoding the
soft-mode-induced positive shift in the activity self-energy) and
the magnitudes agree within a factor of $1.5$.  This is the
typical accuracy of unresummed 2-loop $\eps$-expansion in $d=1$;
LDWC's 0.14331 result, by contrast, sat at much smaller effective
$\eps$ for that calculation.

The structural feature that makes the prediction work: the
$\chi$-$\chi'$ contraction in the activity self-energy routes
through a response-field $\rho$-$\tilde\rho$ propagator, contributing
to the activity anomalous dimension with the \emph{opposite sign}

\paragraph{The full primary-exponent set via the Le Doussal--Wiese

$\tau$ depends on the full exponent set $(\beta, \nu_\perp,

this set is \emph{not} DP with a small conservation correction:
\citet{LeDoussalWiese2015} proved that the stochastic field theory
for CDP maps \emph{exactly} onto the quenched Edwards--Wilkinson
(qEW) depinning transition, via
\begin{equation}
  n(x, t) = n_0 + \nabla^2 u(x, t),
  \label{eq:LDW-map}
\end{equation}

interface position.  Under~\eqref{eq:LDW-map}, the CDP activity
corresponds to the interface velocity, and all CDP critical
exponents are determined by the qEW exponents $(\zeta, z)$
through the standard depinning scaling relations.

rank-$k$ argument and the depinning correlation length.

\paragraph{A methodological warning.}
\label{par:warning}
We include this warning because an earlier version of this
subsection pursued a different route that looked quantitatively
successful but was physically wrong.  The alternative framing
treated CDP as ``DP plus a small $O(B_3 \eps^2)$ conservation
correction.''  That framing predicted $\tau = 1.290$, matching
the measured $1.29$ to three decimals, and also hit $z, \beta$
within $5\%$.  It looked like a result.

It was not.  The apparent success came from three compounding
coincidences.  (i) Measured Manna $(z, \beta, \tau)$ happen to
sit within $10\%$ of measured DP $(z, \beta, \tau)$ in $1+1$d, so
any theory reproducing DP automatically reproduces Manna for
those three.  (ii) The ``predicted'' $\tau = 1.290$ came from
applying a hyperscaling relation to internally inconsistent
$(\beta, \nu_\perp)$ inputs whose errors cancelled in the ratio
$\beta/\nu_\perp$.  (iii) The exponents where Manna genuinely
departs from DP --- $\nu_\perp$ ($1.35$ vs DP $1.10$) and
$\delta$ ($0.14$ vs DP $0.16$) --- were predicted badly by the
wrong framework ($\nu_\perp = 0.88$; $\delta = 0.24$), and we
\emph{initially rationalised} those as ``known Pad\'e limitations
at large $\eps$'' rather than recognising them as the diagnostic.

The deprecated implementation is retained in the repository at
\texttt{rdft/ac/manna\_dp\_anchor\_DEPRECATED.py} for the
methodological record.

\emph{The safeguard we now use}: a CFAC prediction must pass a
\textbf{joint-exponent consistency check} --- at least $k$
independent exponents must simultaneously fall within stated
tolerances, \emph{and} the residuals must be distributed, not
concentrated in a single combination.  A match on $\tau$ alone
(which is a ratio of ratios) is not a test; a match on
$\{\nu_\perp, \delta, \beta\}$ independently is.  The LDW
qEW mapping passes this check (3 of 5 within $2\sigma$ individually,
with residuals distributed across the exponents and no
compensating cancellations).  The DP-anchor framing fails it
(concentrated error on $\nu_\perp$, apparent success driven by
ratio cancellation).  The test
\texttt{test\_dp\_anchor\_fails\_nu\_perp} enforces this
distinction at the repository level: a future revision of the
DP-anchor approach cannot accidentally be reintroduced without
breaking that test.

The deeper lesson is classical: at $\eps=3$ perturbation theory is
non-perturbative, and a single hit is not a prediction.  We
recommend reporting five or more independent observables with
individual residuals before claiming a theory has captured a
universality class.

\paragraph{The remaining gap.}
Two pieces of the slotting are now present: the codimension
argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:codim}, structural) and the
$\gamma_3$ scale prediction
($\tau_{\rm pred} = 1.33 - 3 B_3 \eps^2 \approx 1.327$, agrees with
observation to $2\%$).  What remains genuinely unfinished:

\textbf{(a) Demonstrating $k=3$ dominance from a microscopic DSE.}
The soft-mode-augmented polynomial DSE in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi_augmented} captures the Ward-identity \emph{tying}
$g_{\rm eff} \propto \chi^2$ but does not by itself promote
$k_{\rm dom}$ to $3$.  This is consistent with Banderier--Drmota:
polynomial DSEs with positive-coefficient structure are stuck on
the dyadic ladder $\{2, 4, 8, \ldots\}$.  The conservation Ward
identity needs to be encoded as a SIGNED constraint on the algebraic
curve --- equivalently, a non-local effective interaction
$\chi^2 \int \psi\tilde\psi\,G_\rho \star \psi\tilde\psi$ where
$\star$ is the diffusive convolution.

\textbf{(b) Computing $n_{\rm eff}$ from first principles.}  The
estimate $n_{\rm eff} = 3$ (symmetric quartic channels) is plausible
on dimensional grounds but not derived from an explicit
1-loop diagram enumeration of the CDP/Manna action.  Different
microscopic realisations of the universality class may give
slightly different $n_{\rm eff}$.

Both are tractable extensions to the existing CFAC infrastructure
but lie beyond what \texttt{conserved\_2.py} currently does.  The
result we have is the \emph{structural} slotting: the codimension
argument plus the bridge-scale prediction are mutually reinforcing
and pass empirical tests, but a fully algebraic demonstration that
the conservation-projected DSE has $k_{\rm dom} = 3$ awaits the
non-local-projector extension.

\section{Directions we believe do \emph{not} currently slot}
\label{sec:boundary}
% ==================================================================

Scouting four further problems from the CFAC candidate
list~\citep[\texttt{docs/problems.md}]{AmarteifioProblems2026}
returned clear structural reasons why the current stratification
framework does not reach them.  These are not proven no-gos: each
flags a specific extension that would be required.  We state them
here to mark the boundary of the framework honestly.

\subsection{LERW in $d=3$}

Loop-erased random walks in $d=3$ have a scaling exponent
$\nu \approx 1.624$ that has resisted closed form since
Kozma~\citep{Kozma2007} proved it is not equal to $3/2$.
Wilson's algorithm connects LERW to spanning-tree enumeration,
i.e.\ to the Kirchhoff polynomial that CFAC computes natively.
However:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item In the thermodynamic limit the LERW generating function
    on $\mathbb{Z}^3$ is a lattice Green's function, not algebraic
    and not $D$-finite; Theorem~\ref{thm:strat} does not apply.
  \item Setting $\tau = \nu$ would require $k = 1/(\nu-1) \approx
    1.60$, neither integer nor dyadic.
\end{itemize}
We believe LERW 3D is not reachable without extending the
stratification framework to non-$D$-finite generating functions.
A legitimate adjacent calculation would be the tropical Kirchhoff
decomposition of Wilson-weight expectations on \emph{finite}
graphs.

\subsection{TAP complexity in spin glasses}

The log-count of critical points of a random spin-glass energy
landscape --- the Bray--Moore / Crisanti--Leuzzi
complexity~\citep{BrayMoore1980,CrisantiLeuzzi2005}, sharpened by
Auffinger--Ben Arous--\v{C}ern\'y~\citep{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}
and Subag~\citep{Subag2017} for pure $p$-spin --- is controlled by
the Kac--Rice formula $\mathbb{E}|\det(H - f I)|$, where $H$ is the
Hessian, an $N\times N$ GOE matrix.
The CFAC bridge function~\citep[Section 4]{AmarteifioCFAC2026} is
scalar: it handles mass-independent scalar self-energies and rank-$k$
polynomial integrands.  The object that would describe TAP complexity
is a \emph{matrix-valued} bridge --- the operator generalisation
whose scalar limit recovers Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge_rank_k} but whose
determinantal structure captures the GOE spectral measure.
We believe this is a genuine extension requiring new mathematics
(free probability, $R$-transforms), not a mechanical tensor dressing
of the existing bridge.
TAP complexity is parked pending that extension.

\subsection{KPZ upper-tail universality}

The KPZ equation in 1+1d has an upper-tail rate function of shape
$|h|^{3/2}$ with a coefficient known only for integrable weight
distributions~\citep{CorwinGhosal2020,Krajenbrink}.
Universality beyond integrable weights is one of the top open
problems in integrable probability.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Structural match: $\tau = 3/2$ is exactly the $\Cstr{2}$
    skeleton (square-root branch), the generic Puiseux class of a
    polynomial DSE.  The tilted-DSE machinery in \texttt{tilted.py}
    handles SCGFs of polynomial DSEs.  Reproducing the
    \emph{exponent} is, structurally, cheap.
  \item Missing machinery: the \emph{coefficient} requires a bridge
    function for disorder-averaged random-weight transfer matrices.
    No such bridge is currently implemented; the existing bridges
    cover deterministic Doi--Peliti + MSR only.
\end{itemize}
We believe KPZ upper-tail universality is a ``cautious go'' for the
scoping layer (show that $\tau = 3/2$ comes out of the tilted
directed-polymer DSE at the $\Cstr{2}$ stratum) and a research
programme for the full universality proof.

\subsection{Why listing these matters}
The stratification theorem, as stated, is narrow: it applies to
polynomial DSEs whose generating functions are algebraic or
$D$-finite.  Each negative case above identifies a specific
structural feature (non-$D$-finite GF, matrix-valued bridge,
disorder-averaged bridge) that the current framework does not
handle.  We list them here rather than in a separate note because
the positive result (Manna/C-DP on $\Cstr{3}$) is only
interpretable against the clear boundary of what the framework
currently covers.

\section{Universal scope: CRN-in-field as the underlying object}
\label{sec:scope}
% ==================================================================

We have framed the Manna calculation in narrow terms (one
universality class, one stratum), but the machinery used here is
not Manna-specific.  This section makes the wider scope claim
explicit so that the Manna result can be read as one specific test
of it.

\paragraph{The scope claim.}
\emph{Any statistical-physics system whose dynamics admits a
Doi--Peliti / MSR action representation --- a chemical reaction
network embedded in a (possibly non-equilibrium) field, with
retarded propagators where appropriate --- falls within the CFAC
analytic framework.}  The two-step procedure is:
\textbf{(i)} write the CRN-in-field representation of the system;
\textbf{(ii)} run the CFAC pipeline (filter diagrams by causal
contraction and bridge factorisation, compute Symanzik polynomials,
apply BPHZ multiplicities $K_G|_{\alpha=1}$, sector-decompose, and
extract the residual list of free integrals).

\paragraph{Demonstrated reach.}
The CFAC pipeline as it stands has been exercised across a
heterogeneous list of systems:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{LDWC depinning}: 2-loop FRG for an elastic manifold,
    multi-field MSR action, doubled-propagator hat
    diagram.~\citep{Amarteifio2026LDWC}
  \item \textbf{LDWC long-range elasticity}: same pipeline,
    fractional propagator exponents.
  \item \textbf{LDWC periodic CDW}: same pipeline, $\Nres = 0$.
  \item \textbf{Wilson--Fisher O($N$) $\eta$ at 2-loop}: equilibrium
    $\phi^4$, no FRG.  Reproduces $\eta = (N{+}2)/[2(N{+}8)^2]\eps^2$.
  \item \textbf{Directed percolation 2-loop}: pure
    Doi--Peliti.~\citep{Amarteifio2026TwoLoop}
  \item \textbf{Manna / C-DP}: this paper (multi-field, conserved,
    non-equilibrium).
\end{itemize}
None of these are in the same physics class.  What they share is
the CRN-in-field structure.  This is evidence for the scope claim,
not proof.

\paragraph{Routes from common stat-phys to CRN-in-field.}
\begin{description}
  \item[Reaction--diffusion:] Direct (Doi--Peliti).
  \item[Equilibrium $\phi^4$ / O($N$):] Stochastic quantisation
    (Parisi--Wu) gives a Langevin equation; its MSR action is a
    CRN-in-field.  The field-theoretic anomalous dimension $\eta$
    falls out of the same pipeline as Manna's $\tau$.
  \item[Spin systems:] Glauber dynamics is literally a CRN of
    spin flips; mapping to a continuous order parameter is standard.
  \item[KPZ / surface growth:] MSR action for the height field;
    the noise vertex defines a CRN.
  \item[Hydrodynamic fluctuations:] MSR for Navier--Stokes; the
    pressure constraint is a Lagrange-multiplier field; the noise
    is a CRN.
  \item[Lattice gauge (stochastic quantisation):] Langevin in field
    space, MSR action with gauge-fixing constraints.
\end{description}
The technical step in each case is well-known; CFAC inherits the
machinery once the action is in standard form.

\paragraph{Where the scope claim genuinely doesn't reach (yet).}
The boundary cases of Section~\ref{sec:boundary} sharpen this:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Matrix-valued fields} (TAP complexity, RMT,
    replica $n\to 0$): the CRN is matrix-valued, and the bridge
    function in CFAC is currently scalar.  A matrix-valued bridge
    (free probability / R-transforms) is a research extension.
  \item \textbf{Non-$D$-finite generating functions} (LERW in
    $d=3$, lattice Green's functions): the stratification theorem
    requires algebraic or $D$-finite GFs.
  \item \textbf{Topologically constrained systems} (BRST-quantised
    gauge theory, field theories with global anomalies): the
    constraint algebra is not currently handled by the pipeline.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Manna's role in the wider programme.}
Manna is deliberately a stress test for the multi-field, conserved,
non-equilibrium corner of the scope.  If CFAC handles Manna
successfully (the slotting holds, the rank-3 bridge gives the right
order of magnitude, and the conservation projector in the multi-field
DSE selects $\Cstr{3}$ as predicted), it is direct evidence that
the wider scope claim is plausible: \emph{a representative system from a
distinct stat-phys corner falls in line with the same machinery}.
A separate note in preparation will state the scope claim formally
and tabulate the demonstrated cases as a reference artefact.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ==================================================================

\paragraph{Summary of the solid contributions.}
Three things are established by this paper and the companion
\citep{Amarteifio2026LDWC}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Stratum classification.} The CFAC codimension
    argument (Prop.~\ref{prop:codim}) places CDP/Manna on the
    $\Cstr{3}$ Puiseux stratum, giving a skeleton
    $\tau_0 = 4/3$ within $3\%$ of the measured $\tau \approx
    1.29$.  This is an organising principle for non-DP
    universality that does not require knowledge of the
    underlying field theory.
  \item \textbf{Cross-check via two independent routes.}  The
    rank-3 skeleton value $4/3 = 1 + 1/k$ \emph{equals}
    $\nu_\perp = 1/(2 - \zeta) = 1/(2 - 5/4) = 4/3$ read from
    the qEW mapping with $\zeta = 5/4$.  Two independent
    derivations --- combinatorial stratification and
    depinning field theory --- land on the same rational.
  \item \textbf{Three of four qEW anchor numbers CFAC-derived.}
    The LDW mapping~\citep{LeDoussalWiese2015} converts
    qEW $(\zeta, z)$ into the full Manna exponent set.  Three
    of the four 2-loop anchor numbers are now reached
    end-to-end from CFAC primitives: $\zeta_1 = 1/3$, $z_1 =
    -2/9$, and $c_\zeta = 0.14331$ (the last to relative error
    $2\times 10^{-4}$, in the companion paper).  Only the
    2-loop dynamical coefficient $c_z$ remains external
    input, with the derivation route flagged.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is \emph{not} claimed.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item We do not claim a first-principles derivation of the
    Manna exponents from within CFAC.  The qEW mapping is the
    proper field theory \citep{LeDoussalWiese2015}; CFAC
    reaches the anchor numbers that feed it.
  \item We do not claim the $\Cstr{3}$ stratum prediction is
    proven from a microscopic DSE.  The conservation Ward
    identity encoding as a signed projector on the algebraic
    curve is the open gap flagged in
    Section~\ref{sec:gamma3} and remains so.
  \item We do not claim a better numerical match to the Manna
    measurements than the measurements internally support.
    The published 1+1d Manna exponents imply three different
    values of $z$ depending on which scaling route is used
    (spread $14\%$); our prediction sits inside that spread.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Methodological contribution.}
The paper documents a wrong-path framing (CDP as DP-plus-small
correction) that produced apparent quantitative success through
ratio cancellation in hyperscaling.  The deprecated implementation
is retained in the repository with a regression test that would
flag any accidental reintroduction.  The joint-exponent
consistency rule proposed in Section~\ref{par:warning} is the
explicit safeguard.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
(i) Port the LDWC $X^{(\alpha)}$ machinery to compute the
short-range qEW dynamical coefficient $c_z$ at 2-loop, closing
the last external input.
(ii) Encode the conservation Ward identity as a signed projector
on the algebraic curve to derive $\Cstr{3}$ stratum dominance
microscopically.
(iii) Test the slotting on a further non-DP class not used in the
hypothesis formation (parity-conserving branching annihilation
random walks, voter class at $k \to \infty$ with log
corrections).

\section{Related coupled-field systems: ants (TODO)}
\label{app:ants}
% ==================================================================

A natural sibling of CDP/Manna in the CFAC programme is the
chemotactic ant colony, treated in~\citep{AmarteifioAnts2026} as the
canonical coupled-field system: a Doi--Peliti particle sector
(ants in two states $S \rightleftharpoons R$) coupled to an
MSR continuous-field sector (pheromone density).  The ant
construction shares the multi-field, non-equilibrium structure that
made CDP/Manna the test case in this paper.

The two systems sit on \emph{different} Puiseux strata, however:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item CDP/Manna: $\Cstr{3}$ (skeleton $\tau_0 = 4/3$, this paper).
  \item Ants: $\Cstr{2}$ saddle-node, with order parameter
    exponent $\beta = 1/2$ verified to $2.7\%$ by simulation
    (\citep[\S 4]{AmarteifioAnts2026}).  Conservation enters
    differently --- the ant ``conservation'' is the total particle
    number, which fixes $\langle S + R\rangle$ but does not generate
    the same Ward identity that promotes CDP from $\Cstr{2}$ to
    $\Cstr{3}$.
\end{itemize}

The two cases together would be a stronger test of the codimension
argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:codim}) than either alone:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{TODO 1.}  Apply the \texttt{conserved\_2.py} (and
    eventual \texttt{conserved\_3.py}) machinery to the ant DSE
    and verify that it returns $\Cstr{2}$ dominant, with $\beta = 1/2$
    falling out of the rank-2 bridge as in standard DP.
  \item \textbf{TODO 2.}  Identify the precise reason the ant
    conservation does \emph{not} promote to $\Cstr{3}$: is it that
    the ant's $S+R$ conservation is an exact lattice constraint
    rather than a Ward identity at the field-theoretic level?
    Or that the conservation acts on the activity sector itself
    rather than coupling activity to a separate conserved background?
  \item \textbf{TODO 3.}  Construct a modified ant model that adds a
    \emph{second} conservation law (e.g., a conserved environmental
    field) and check whether the codimension argument predicts $\Cstr{4}$
    promotion, observing what numerical $\beta$ emerges.
\end{itemize}

These cross-tests would either strengthen the slotting framework
(by showing it correctly predicts \emph{which} coupled-field systems
sit on which strata) or expose its failure mode (a coupled-field
system with conservation that nonetheless does not slot as
predicted).  Both outcomes are informative.  We defer this work to a
follow-up note.
